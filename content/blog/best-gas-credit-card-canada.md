Gas rewards look simple because the category is easy to understand: you buy fuel, your card gives you cash back or points.

But the **best gas credit card in Canada** is not necessarily the card with the largest percentage beside the word "gas."

Annual fees, reward caps, merchant coding, where you actually fill up and what the card earns on the rest of your spending can all change the result.

The useful question is:

**Which card gives me the highest net annual value from my real fuel spending and the rest of my budget?**

## The Short Answer

If you spend heavily on gas, an accelerated gas rate can be valuable. Current Canadian examples include cards such as the **TD Cash Back Visa Infinite**, which advertises 3% cash back on eligible gas and EV charging purchases, and other cards that offer elevated gas rewards as part of broader everyday-spending packages.

But a 3% gas card with an annual fee can still lose to a no-fee 2% flat-rate card if your gas spending is low.

The break-even math matters more than the headline rate.

## Start With Your Annual Fuel Spending

Suppose you spend **$300 per month on gas**.

That is:

**$300 × 12 = $3,600 per year**

At different reward rates:

| Gas reward rate | Approx. annual rewards on $3,600 |
| --- | ---: |
| 1% | $36 |
| 2% | $72 |
| 3% | $108 |
| 4% | $144 |

The difference between 1% and 3% is **$72 per year**.

If the 3% card charges a $120 annual fee and does not give you additional value elsewhere, gas rewards alone do not justify the fee.

That is the first calculation to make.

## Why a Higher Gas Rate Can Still Lose

Imagine two cards.

### Card A

- $0 annual fee
- 2% cash back on all purchases

### Card B

- $120 annual fee
- 4% on gas
- 1% on everything else

If you spend $3,600 per year on gas:

Card A earns:

**$3,600 × 2% = $72**

Card B earns:

**$3,600 × 4% = $144**

The extra gas reward is only **$72**.

That does not recover the $120 annual fee by itself.

If Card B also earns more on groceries, recurring bills or transit, it may still win overall. But the gas category alone is not enough.

## Find the Gas Spending Break-Even Point

If one card earns 2 percentage points more on gas than another and costs $120 per year, the break-even calculation is:

**$120 ÷ 0.02 = $6,000 per year in gas spending**

That is about **$500 per month**.

If your annual fuel spending is far below that, the gas multiplier may not be the reason to choose the card.

## TD Cash Back Visa Infinite as an Example

TD currently advertises **3% cash back on eligible gas and EV charging purchases** on the TD Cash Back Visa Infinite.

The same card also advertises 3% on categories such as groceries, public transit, recurring bill payments and certain streaming/digital purchases, subject to stated annual category limits.

That broader structure is important.

A card may not need to be the absolute best at gas if it also performs strongly in several other large categories in your budget.

For someone spending heavily on gas, groceries and recurring bills, one well-rounded card can sometimes be more useful than optimizing each category separately.

## Gas Station Loyalty Programs Can Change the Result

Some fuel retailers have loyalty programs that can be combined with credit-card rewards.

That means your effective return can come from two layers:

**credit-card reward + station loyalty reward**

If you already prefer a particular chain, this can influence which card is best for you.

But do not switch to a more expensive gas station just to earn extra points.

A 2% credit-card reward is worth **2 cents per dollar**. If the station charges several cents more per litre, the higher fuel price can wipe out the reward quickly.

The cheapest net fuel cost matters more than collecting the most points.

## Merchant Coding Still Matters

Accelerated gas rewards usually depend on the merchant being classified as an eligible gas station or automated fuel dispenser by the card network.

That means purchases made inside a convenience store attached to a gas station may not always be treated the same way as fuel purchased at the pump.

Likewise, warehouse-club gas stations can have different payment-network and merchant-coding considerations.

## What About EV Charging?

Some newer Canadian reward cards explicitly include eligible electric-vehicle charging transactions in their accelerated gas or transportation category.

If you are transitioning from gasoline to an EV, check whether the card's terms include EV charging rather than assuming a traditional gas category covers it.

This is becoming increasingly relevant because a card chosen specifically for gasoline may be less useful as your transportation spending changes.

## Spending Caps Can Reduce the Advertised Rate

A card may advertise 3% or 4% on gas but limit the amount of annual spending that receives the accelerated rate.

Once the cap is reached, additional fuel purchases may earn only the base rate.

For most households, gas spending alone may not reach a high cap. But if the limit is shared across multiple categories, your grocery, transit or recurring-bill spending can use up the same reward bucket.

Always check how the cap is defined.

See our guide to [credit card earn-rate caps in Canada](/blog/credit-card-earn-rate-caps-canada) for more detail.

## Gas Rewards Versus Flat-Rate Cash Back

Flat-rate cards are often underrated for fuel purchases.

If a no-fee card gives you 2% on almost everything, you already have a strong baseline.

A premium gas card earning 3% only adds another 1 percentage point.

At $4,000 of annual gas spending, that extra 1% is only **$40 per year**.

That does not mean the 3% card is bad. It means the rest of the card has to justify the decision.

## Who Benefits Most From a Gas-Focused Card?

A strong gas rewards card is most valuable for people who:

- drive long distances regularly;
- commute daily by car;
- operate multiple household vehicles;
- spend heavily on fuel for work;
- combine high gas spending with other bonus categories on the same card;
- or can stack the card with a station loyalty program they already use.

For someone who spends $80 per month on gas, optimizing another category may have a much larger impact.

## Compare Gas in the Context of Your Whole Budget

If you spend:

- $250 per month on gas;
- $800 on groceries;
- $400 on dining;
- and $500 on recurring bills,

then gas is only one part of the decision.

A card that earns 4% on gas but 1% everywhere else could lose to a card that earns 3% on gas and several other categories.

## Bottom Line

The best gas credit card in Canada is not simply the card with the highest gas percentage.

Start with your annual fuel spending, calculate the extra reward versus your best alternative, subtract the annual fee and then include the card's value across the rest of your spending.

If the card still wins after that, the gas multiplier is actually working for you.

**Optimize your total return — not just the number printed beside the gas pump icon.**
